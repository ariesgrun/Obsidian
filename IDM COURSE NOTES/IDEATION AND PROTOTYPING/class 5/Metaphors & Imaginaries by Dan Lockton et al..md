- research to on design that changes behavior is underexplored
- 3 projects on energy use, life and career, and generating new metaphors will adress these areas
- goal: seek to understand peoples' understanding better
- “How do we understand?” is becoming increasingly important as we become enmeshed in complex systems of nature, technology and society, from ecosystems to AI to big data to our own health.
- Design working on behaviour change has so far largely ignored this dimension,
- This paper briefly introduces three projects which explore considering metaphors and imaginaries more widely almost as a ‘material’ for design research,

ENERGY
- People questioned how they could change how they use energy when they can’t easily see or feel it, or get a sense of the changing rate at which it is being used.
- ‘What does energy look like?’, - leading to possible design implications for new forms of energy feedback or display
-
Mental Landscapes
- Landscapes are a common type of metaphor in speech, particularly for talking about relations between parts of a whole, or mapping the structure of one concept onto another. In organizations, we might talk about moving into new territory or the stakeholder landscape, having a vantage point, mainstream and backwater, channelling our efforts, the lay of the land, descending into chaos, oceans of possibilities—even blue sky thinking.
- “What do the past and future look like as a landscape?”
- For example, different metaphors used by participants could inspire a new form of interface design for life planning or project-management tools.
