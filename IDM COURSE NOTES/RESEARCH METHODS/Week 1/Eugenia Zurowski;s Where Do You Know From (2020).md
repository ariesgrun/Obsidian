Notes
- Foundations in Cultural Studies and Critical Theory exercise
	- shared the exercise informally online (helpful - from prof)
- importance of attending conscientiously to the ways we relate to one another in the classroom as part of our pedagogical and political responsibilities as instructors.
- mundane academic structures -> colonialist hierarchies of power and alienate Indigenous students
	-  disregards their ways of knowing and being
- McKittrick taught her to replace the question ‘Where are you from?’ with ‘Where do you know from?’ or ‘Where do you learn from?’
- separate the question of who we _are_ from the question of what we know and what we are here to learn.
- Academic intellectual authority—what we think it looks, sounds, and feels like; where we think it _comes from_—is precisely the problem,
- I use this exercise to give my students the opportunity to tell me and each other what kinds of knowing are present, so we are in a position to learn from one another.

EXERCISE
- 5–10 minutes to read through it and reflect on the prompts,
- spend the next 10 minutes introducing themselves to the person sitting next to them at the table in terms of ‘where they know from’.
- We then go around the table and introduce ourselves to the entire group.

Questions
1. **What is your name?**
2. **Which pronouns would you like us to use in reference to you?**
3. **What are your intellectual interests?**
	1. What do you think about a lot?
	2. What have you learned about?
	3. Have you read or watched or heard something lately
4. **How did your interests come to you?**
	1. how would you narrate what brought you to your ideas, or your ideas to you?
	2. Was it something you read, witnessed, confronted?
	3. taught to you?
	4. somewhere you lived or went, someone you met or knew, something you laboured at, something you enjoyed?
5. **What is your intellectual work for?**
	1. question both about your motivations for enrolling in this degree program and taking this course
	2. What are you hoping to accomplish in the course of completing your degree?
	3. hope to learn
6. **What else would you like us to remember and recognize about you when we engage in conversation with you?**
	1. is there anything you haven’t yet mentioned, or that you have but would like to emphasise?

- we bring different experiences and different sets of learning into the classroom. 
- listen to the various kinds of knowledge, curiosity, and objectives that the group has brought to the table.
- This diversity will give shape to the intellectual work we pursue collectively over the course of the semester.
